01 / The opportunityWhy this matters

AI is changing products.
Design must catch up.

Participants will tackle emerging AI interaction and product challenges, including how people understand, guide and trust increasingly autonomous systems.

The goal is a functional prototype that people can experience, supported by project visuals and a short demo video.

Product thinking

Real enterprise questions

Potential briefs explore multi-agent interaction, understandable AI systems and new interfaces for autonomous software.

Final briefs will be announced before the event.
Tangible outputs

Concepts made visible

Functional prototypes, project visuals and demos make student ideas tangible and shareable.

02 / The programOnline → San Francisco

From concept
to live demo.

  1. Nov 7–9

    Online makeathon, panel & workshops

    Three days of team formation, an industry panel, hands-on workshops, design and prototyping around real-world AI challenges.

  2. Nov 10–12

    Online judging

    A three-day virtual judging window: judges work in pairs, one from design and one with deep business and user context, and each pair reviews a group of projects; final format and criteria to be confirmed.

    Winner Demo

    The only in-person day: selected winning teams present at Third Coast Foundry in San Francisco, followed by awards and a mixer. Everything before it is virtual.

04 / Ways to partnerShaped with each team

Bring your
team in.

Partnership starts with what your team wants to share. These are the ways companies take part, and we shape the details together. Taking part is free.

Host

Run a session for students

An hour of your time, a lot of their learning. Students rarely get to see how design works inside a company. A 45-minute online session during Nov 7–9 gives them that, and gives your team a live room of future designers. Pick a format, or mix the two.

How you build

A day in the life of a product designer on your team, and how a design idea moves from brief to shipped product: the tools, the trade-offs, the reviews.

Getting hired

What you look for in interns, what the roles involve, and honest job-hunting advice for new grads. Share open roles if you have them.

Judging

Send a judge

Nominate a designer, product manager or engineer for the panel. Judges work in pairs, one from design and one with business and user context, and each pair reviews a group of projects during Nov 10–12.

Office hours

Open an office hour

A drop-in slot where teams bring a concept, a prototype or a portfolio and get feedback from people who ship products for a living.

Tools

Give teams something to build with

Tool access, credits or resources that participants can use during the three days. In-kind contributions are welcome and credited.

Share

Spread the word

Tell designers in your network, your alumni group or your campus contacts. We provide the poster and ready-to-share copy.

These formats are proposals; timing and scope are agreed with each team. A small number of sponsors fund prizes and can bring a real business scenario as a dedicated track; if that interests you, ask us. Neither partnership nor sponsorship influences judging or award decisions.

06 / QuestionsBefore you reply

Good to
know.

The short answers to what partner teams usually ask first.

Is there a cost?

No. Running a session, judging, office hours and in-kind tools are free. Sponsorship, which funds prizes, is separate and optional.

How much time does it take?

About an hour for a session: 45 minutes live plus a short prep call. We handle scheduling, the platform and promotion.

Can we recruit?

Yes. Share your open internship and new-grad roles with participants. Introductions to individual students need their consent.

Does partnering affect judging?

No. Judging criteria and decisions are independent of partnership and sponsorship.
